Small proteins (SPs), typically under 100 amino acids, are essential yet chronically under-characterized components of microbial biology. Encoded by small open reading frames (smORFs), these proteins participate in signaling, stress response, and regulatory processes, yet their short length and rapid sequence divergence render homology-based annotation largely ineffective. This is especially problematic in metagenomic datasets, where assembly fragmentation and low coverage hinder detection, leaving most predicted smORFs functionally uncharacterized.

This project addresses this gap by developing synteny-based computational frameworks that infer smORF function from conserved genomic context rather than sequence similarity alone. As proof of concept, GMSC-mapper was applied to 52 canine faecal metagenomes from the long-read Shanghai Dog Cohort, identifying over one million smORF occurrences collapsing into hundreds of thousands of non-redundant peptides and representative families. Analysis revealed substantial inter-sample variability alongside a small core of highly conserved sequences across all samples. A synteny-based pipeline extracts gene neighborhoods around each smORF, annotating neighboring genes via EggNOG-mapper and COG classification to reveal recurring genomic contexts suggestive of function. A permutation-based statistical framework, using contig-level gene-order randomization, distinguishes non-random syntenic signals from background structure, supported by odds-ratio and Jaccard-similarity metrics.

The validated framework will be scaled to the SPIRE database, spanning tens of thousands of metagenomic samples and billions of predicted open reading frames, testing the generality of synteny-based inference across taxa, habitats, and sequencing technologies. The resulting open-source tool will map predicted smORFs against reference catalogs, including the Global Microbial Small Proteins Catalog (GMSC), which aggregates hundreds of millions of non-redundant smORF sequences, using conserved genomic neighborhoods to generate ranked functional hypotheses, transforming unannotated smORF catalogs into structured, interpretable resources for validation and biotechnological applications.
